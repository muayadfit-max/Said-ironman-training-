1. Running
Run/walk method for complete beginners
The Run/Walk Method

As a complete beginner, run/walk intervals are your best tool. Do not try to run continuously — it leads to injury and burnout. These intervals are exactly how elite coaches build every new runner.

Week 1 Walk 2 min → Run 1 min · Repeat 5–6×
Week 2 Walk 2 min → Run 2 min · Repeat 5–6×
Week 3 Walk 1 min → Run 3 min · Repeat 4–5×
Week 4 Walk 1 min → Run 5 min · Repeat 4–5×
Form Basics
1
Head up, eyes forward — look 10–15 m ahead, not at your feet. This opens your airway and keeps your posture upright.
2
Relax your shoulders — drop them away from your ears. Tension here wastes energy and tires you out faster.
3
Short, quick steps — aim for your foot landing under your hips, not out in front. Shorter strides reduce injury risk dramatically.
4
Arms at ~90° — drive elbows back (not across your body). Your arms set the pace for your legs.
✅ Always warm up with 5 min brisk walking before your first run interval.
✅ Cool down with 5 min walking and gentle stretching after.
✅ Invest in proper running shoes — this is the single best investment you can make. Ask at a specialist running store for a gait analysis.

2. Swimming
From 25 m to confident open-water swimmer
🌊 25 metres feels impossible right now. That is completely normal and expected. Swimming is 80% technique and breathing — not fitness. Your fitness is already building through running and resistance. Focus on breathing first, distance second.
Week-by-Week Plan
Weeks 1 & 2
4–6 × 25 m with full rest (60–90 sec). Rest at the wall as long as you need — no shame. Target: 100–150 m total per session.
Weeks 3 & 4
4–6 × 50 m with 60 sec rest. Try 1–2 continuous 100 m sets. Target: 200–300 m total per session.
Breathing — The Key Skill
1
Exhale underwater — blow air out through your nose and mouth while your face is in the water. Many beginners forget to exhale and then gasp when they turn.
2
Breathe every 2–3 strokes — turn your head to the side (not up). Your ear stays in the water. Inhale quickly, face back down.
3
Don't fight the water — relax. Tension makes you sink. The water wants to support you if you let it.
3 Beginner Drills
🦵 Kick with board: Hold a kickboard, focus only on your leg kick. Builds feel for propulsion.

💪 Single-arm freestyle: One arm extended, stroke with the other. Slows everything down so you can feel your technique.

🤲 Catch-up drill: Wait until the leading hand is fully extended before the other arm starts its stroke. Teaches timing and glide.

Current Program: Indoor Gym Bike

For the entire 4-week program (May 10 – June 6, 2026), all cycling sessions are done indoors at the gym on a stationary bike. This is the perfect starting environment for a beginner — it's safe, fully controlled, and lets you focus entirely on building time in the saddle without worrying about traffic, road conditions, or balance on a new bike.

🎯 Your only job on the gym bike: build your legs and your aerobic engine. Use the bike's display to track your time, distance, and resistance level. Log everything in the app.
Bike Fit — Even on a Stationary Bike

Getting the setup right prevents knee pain and makes riding far more efficient. Spend 2 minutes adjusting before every session.

1
Seat height: When your foot is at the bottom of the pedal stroke (6 o'clock position), your knee should have a slight bend — not fully straight and not deeply bent. Stand next to the bike: the seat should be at hip height.
2
Handlebar height: Keep the bars high enough so you're not hunching over or straining your neck. For beginners, handlebar at or slightly above seat level is comfortable and safe.
3
Foot position: The ball of your foot (widest part) should be over the centre of the pedal. If the gym bike has toe cages, use them — they help prevent your foot from slipping.
Building Time in the Saddle

The goal for Weeks 3 and 4 is simple: easy, steady effort for longer periods. Resist the urge to push hard. Endurance is built at low-to-moderate intensity, not by going all-out.

Week 3 · Sat First dedicated cycling session. 20–25 min easy. Set resistance to a light-to-moderate level. If you break a light sweat and can hold a conversation, you're at the perfect intensity.
Week 4 · Sat 25–30 min with 2–3 short "harder effort" intervals: pedal harder for 30 seconds, then easy for 2 minutes. Repeat 2–3 times within the session.
Cadence: 80–90 RPM

RPM = Revolutions Per Minute (how many times you complete a full pedal circle in one minute). Aim for 80–90 RPM — this is the sweet spot that protects your knees and builds cycling efficiency.

How to check without an RPM display: Count how many times your right knee comes up in 15 seconds. Multiply by 4. That's your RPM. Aim for 20–23 counts in 15 seconds = 80–92 RPM. ✅
Increasing Intensity Safely
1
Increase duration before resistance. Add 5 minutes to your ride before you add more resistance. Always.
2
Use short intervals. 30 seconds harder effort → 2 minutes easy recovery. Never go all-out for more than 30–45 seconds in these early weeks.
3
The 10% rule. Never increase your total cycling time by more than 10% per week. So if Week 3 is 25 min, Week 4 is maximum 27–28 min. Injuries almost always come from doing too much too soon.
July 2026: Your Road Bike

Starting next month, we will get you a proper outdoor road bike so you can begin real-world cycling. Everything you build on the gym bike — your leg strength, your cadence habit, your cardiovascular base — transfers directly to the road.

🛣️ When we move outdoors, we'll cover: road safety, gear shifting, cornering, climbing, and group riding etiquette. For now, every minute on the gym bike is an investment in that future version of you.

Extra Tips
Recovery, sleep, nutrition timing & hydration
Nutrition Timing
🍽️ Pre-workout (1–2 hrs before): light, easy-to-digest meal. Oats, banana, rice with chicken. Avoid heavy fats or fibre right before training.

🥩 Post-workout (within 30–60 min): this is your protein window. Protein shake + meal is ideal. This is when your body rebuilds muscle most effectively — always hit your 200–230 g daily target.

🍌 During sessions over 60 min: carry a banana or date for quick energy.
Sleep — Non-Negotiable
😴 7–9 hours per night is a training requirement, not a luxury. The majority of your muscle repair, hormonal recovery, and endurance adaptation happens during deep sleep. A poor night's sleep before a session will hurt performance more than any missed workout.
Hydration
💧 2–3 litres of water daily as a baseline. Add 500 ml–1 L for every training session. A simple check: your urine should be pale yellow. Dark yellow = you're dehydrated — drink more.
Recovery Strategies
1
Cold water / contrast shower after training: 2–3 min cold, 1 min warm, repeat 2–3 times. Reduces muscle soreness significantly.
2
Legs up the wall (5–10 min): lie on your back with legs raised against a wall after running or cycling. Drains fluid from legs, reduces aching.
3
If in doubt, rest. Overtraining injuries end training blocks. One extra rest day never hurt anyone. Trying to train through pain always does.
